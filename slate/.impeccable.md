# Impeccable Design Context — Slate

## Design Context

### Users
Mixed professional audience: real estate agents, conveyancers, solicitors (primary); paralegals, admin staff, and small business owners (secondary). All are **task-focused**: they arrive with a specific form to fill and want to complete it quickly and correctly. Context of use is professional — desktop, during a work session, often on deadline. Users are not excited about the software itself; they want it invisible and fast. Legal form errors are costly, so confidence and clarity matter more than delight.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Precise. Confident. Capable.

Slate is a professional productivity tool, not a consumer app. The name itself evokes something clean, architectural, a blank surface ready for work. The tone should feel like a well-made desktop application — not an iPhone app, not a SaaS marketing page. Trustworthy without being corporate. Fast without being flashy.

**Not**: Apple (too borrowed), Linear (too dev-cool), Google Workspace (too soulless), Notion (too casual).
**Reference feel**: The precision of a good CAD tool, the clarity of a Swiss legal document, the confidence of a financial terminal done tastefully.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light. Professional context, daytime use, document-heavy work. Dark would feel wrong for a form-filling tool.
- **Colour identity**: Needs its own identity beyond Apple's exact palette. The accent (#5856D6 iOS indigo) should stay (or close to it) since it's established in the product, but the surrounding neutrals should stop being verbatim Apple HIG.
- **Typography**: Inter must go. It's the single biggest "AI made this" signal. The product should use a type pairing with personality — a slightly geometric or condensed sans for headings/UI, a slightly warmer neutral for body.
- **Anti-references**: The current design looks like every Apple-inspired SaaS template from 2023. Icon-in-rounded-square + heading + description cards repeated in a 3-col grid. Pure Apple neutrals. Inter. It's technically competent but completely unmemorable.

### Design Principles
1. **Workspace first**: The fill workspace is the product. Everything else is preamble. Design decisions should optimise for focused, distraction-free form completion.
2. **Trust through clarity**: Legal forms carry real consequences. Visual hierarchy, clear labelling, and obvious state (what's been filled, what hasn't, what the AI detected) reduce anxiety.
3. **Distinctness without drama**: The product should have a recognisable visual identity without being loud. Subtle typographic choices and a slightly warmer/cooler neutral palette go further than bold colours.
4. **Information density that respects professionals**: Power users filling forms daily need density. Don't over-pad or over-simplify. Labels can be small. Interactions can be efficient. Professionals tolerate learning curves.
5. **No borrowed aesthetics**: Every design decision should be explainable by the product's own needs, not by "this is what Apple does."
